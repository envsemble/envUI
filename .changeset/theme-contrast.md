`Theme` and `ThemePreferenceProvider` take a `contrast`: `high` for the high-contrast colors, `standard` to keep the standard ones even when the system asks for more contrast, or `system` to follow `prefers-contrast: more`. Left out, a Theme takes the contrast of the Theme around it, and `system` outside any. Nested Themes and envUI's popups, such as menus, Select lists, tooltips, toasts and dialogs, keep the contrast of the boundary they open from, as they keep its color scheme. With `applyToDocument`, the provider writes the contrast on the document root too, and removes from it only a contrast it wrote. `createThemeBootstrapScript` takes the same `contrast`, so a fixed high or standard contrast is on the root before the first paint. The script marks what it writes with `data-env-contrast-source="bootstrap"`, and the provider treats that contrast as its own once the application starts: its contrast replaces it, and at `system` it removes it, so a contrast the application stores as a user setting does not stay at the script's value after a reload.

`useTheme()` returns `{ theme, contrast }` instead of the color scheme alone: read the scheme as `const { theme } = useTheme()`, and re-theme an application's own portal with `<Theme {...useTheme()}>`, which the `v1-names` codemod writes for `<Theme theme={useTheme()}>`. In development, where the bundler defines `process.env.NODE_ENV`, Theme reports a `theme` that is not `light` or `dark`; without a bundler it stays silent. Its `contrast` wins over a `data-env-contrast` attribute passed to it; without the prop, such an attribute sets the contrast for the boundary and everything in it.
